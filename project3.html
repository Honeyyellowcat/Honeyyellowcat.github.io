Aerospace Laboratory I

Finite Wing Analysis in the Kirsten Wind Tunnel (KWT)

Summary:

A team investigation in AA 321 Aerospace Laboratory, testing how lift, drag, and pitching moment on a finite wing (NACA 23012, aspect ratio 6) change when circular end caps are added, across two tunnel speeds and an angle-of-attack sweep from −4° to +20° in the Kirsten Wind Tunnel.

1. Finite Wings Report: Here→

Abstract:

- Measured lift, drag, and pitching moment on a NACA 23012 finite wing (aspect ratio 6) in the Kirsten Wind Tunnel, with and without circular end caps.

- Found that end caps reduced the drag coefficient across the angle-of-attack range and modestly increased the stall angle, while the lift-curve slope stayed essentially unchanged.

- Used surface flow visualization (china clay, black light) to confirm reduced wingtip-vortex turbulence with end caps installed.

Introduction:

- Objective: quantify how finite-span effects (wingtip vortices, induced drag) change a wing's lift, drag, and moment, and how circular end caps mitigate those effects.

- Wing: NACA 23012 airfoil, 90 in span, 15 in mean aerodynamic chord, 9.375 ft² reference area (aspect ratio 6).

- Tested at two indicated pressures (10 psf and 35 psf) across an angle-of-attack sweep from −4° to +20°, with and without end caps.

Theory:

- Raw balance readings were resolved into forces with a calibration matrix, then corrected for strut tare and weight tare.

- Finite-wing drag was modeled as parasitic drag plus induced drag, CD = CD,p + CL²/(π·AR·e), where e is the span efficiency factor.

- The 3D lift-curve slope was derived from the 2D slope using the Prandtl lifting-line relation, isolating the lift-slope loss caused by wingtip vortices.

Methods and Procedure:

- Measured wing geometry and marked "trip dot" positions before testing; KWT staff calibrated the angle of attack and ran a strut-tare pass.

- Swept angle of attack from −4° to +10° in 2° steps, then to +20° in 1° steps, then back down, at each of two indicated pressures (10 psf, 35 psf), with and without the circular end caps.

- Ran a china-clay / black-light surface flow visualization at 35 psf to observe wingtip-vortex and separation behavior.

- Reduced the DAQ's raw load-cell data in MATLAB: applied the tare corrections, then the force-balance matrix to get corrected lift, drag, and moment.

Results and Discussion:

- At 10° angle of attack, corrected loads were L ≈ 79.7 lb and D ≈ 5.2 lb — close to the KWT staff's reference values; the corrected moment (≈ −5.77 lb·ft) deviated by about 6.5 lb·ft from their reference, pointing to a likely error in the moment tare or correction rather than in lift or drag.

- With end caps on (10 psf), span efficiency e ≈ 1.15 and parasitic drag coefficient CD,p ≈ 0.0225.

- The measured 3D lift-curve slope (≈ 0.731) was about 0.026 below the 2D slope (≈ 0.757), consistent with the lift loss expected from wingtip vortices on a finite span.

- Flow visualization showed visibly more wingtip turbulence without the end caps, supporting the drag and lift-slope trends.

- Pitching the wing down from 20° to 15° produced hysteresis, most visible in the 35 psf / end-caps-on case.

Conclusion:

- Circular end caps reduced the drag coefficient across the angle-of-attack range and modestly increased the stall angle, while leaving the lift-curve slope essentially unchanged; the moment coefficient increased with end caps at every angle tested.

- Practical takeaway: a comparatively small geometry change (end caps) measurably improved finite-wing performance without a full wing redesign — relevant to low-cost retrofits in real aircraft design.
